## D — Decision question and company-specific line

**What are five years of NVIDIA's statements worth, built from assumptions I can defend?**

NVIDIA's distinctive line is **AI-infrastructure demand under China export controls**. It matters because Data Center demand has made revenue grow rapidly, while the H20 charge and export restrictions show that both the revenue path and gross margin can be disrupted. The model therefore fades revenue growth from 30% to 10% and holds gross margin near 73%, rather than extending the recent triple-digit growth rate. NVIDIA has no dealer inventory financing or floor-plan financing; its filing reports inventories, accounts payable, and debt, but no floor-plan debt. That is the stated **none** for this company.

## I and V — Run, checks, value, and market comparison

Run `python nvda_proforma.py` from this folder. Cash is computed from the cash-flow statement after every non-cash account is forecast; it is never typed as a balancing plug. The check function raises a `ValueError` for any annual balance-sheet gap of $0.05 million or more, or cash below the $10,000 million floor. For example, changing the cash-flow link or an account balance produces the named year and gap rather than allowing valuation to continue.

The model outputs five balanced years and one value per share. Its audited base-case output is **$53.34 per share**. It does not draw a revolver in any forecast year because cash stays above the $10,000 million floor. If any future case did produce negative FCFE, the model would label that year “negative FCFE,” value only positive explicit FCFE, and would not calculate a terminal value from a negative cash flow: a perpetuity of negative cash flow is not a meaningful equity-value number.

As of **September 24, 2026**, the observed NVDA closing price was **$222.82 per share** according to [StockScan's historical-price page](https://stockscan.io/stocks/NVDA/price-history). The model says **$53.34** per share, while the market says **$222.82** on the same FY2026 share count of 24,304 million; what future revenue, margin, capital-return, or discount-rate assumptions explain the difference? This is a valuation question, not a recommendation.

## Reflection

The label I would defend longest is the **history** label on FY2026 inventory days because it is a transparent calculation from two reported statements. The filing number that surprised me most was the $4.0 billion FY2026 inventory provision, which shows that even a fast-growing semiconductor company can face a large inventory risk.
